If you run marketing or sales, account-based marketing (ABM) means picking a short list of companies you want as customers and treating each one as its own market. This guide explains how ABM works, how to choose the accounts, and how to reach the people inside them without a big team.

**Quick summary**

- ABM starts with the companies you want, then finds the people, instead of waiting for leads to arrive.
- Pick accounts from your ideal customer profile, then research each one before anyone writes a word.
- Map the group of people who decide, not one contact.
- Marketing and sales work the same list, and watch the same accounts for signs it's the right moment.

## What is account-based marketing?

Account-based marketing is a way of selling where you choose your target companies first and aim your marketing and sales at them by name.

In practice, that means:

- a short list of accounts, often 20 to 200 to start
- research on each account: what they do, what changed, who decides
- messages written for that company, not for a whole industry
- marketing and sales working from the same list

Here's an example: "Our 50 target accounts are UK insurers with 500 to 5,000 staff. For each one, we know the head of claims, the head of IT and the finance lead, and what each of them cares about."

## Is ABM the same as inbound marketing?

No. Inbound casts a wide net and waits to see who comes. ABM names the companies first and goes to them.

Many teams run both. Inbound brings in the leads you didn't know about, and ABM goes after the ones you do.

## Why does ABM matter?

Because when a few companies matter most, they deserve more than a generic email.

- **No wasted effort on bad fits.** Every ad, email and call goes to a company you chose.
- **Better first conversations.** You know the account before you call, so the first meeting is about them.
- **Sales and marketing pull together.** One list, one set of names, one view of what's happening at each account.
- **Clear results.** You can count how many target accounts replied, met and bought.

## How do you run account-based marketing?

In six steps: pick the accounts, research them, map the people, watch for moments, reach out, and review.

### 1. Pick your target accounts

Start from your [ideal customer profile](/blog/ideal-customer-profile). It tells you which kind of company to pick.

To turn it into names, "[Find companies like your best customers](/missions/lead.lookalikes.find)" takes your customers' websites and returns similar companies, each with a reason it was picked. "[Score companies against your ideal customer](/missions/company.icp.score)" then scores the candidates on fit, so you keep the strongest.

### 2. Research each account

Before anyone writes, know the company. "[Research a company](/missions/company.research)" builds one sourced profile from a company's name or website: its official register record, news, site pages, social page and a written summary.

For a longer list, "[Research up to 100 companies at once](/missions/company.research.bulk)" answers up to three questions for up to 100 companies in one sheet. It needs approval, because it's a bigger spend.

### 3. Map the people who decide

Big purchases are rarely one person's call. "[Map the contacts at a company](/missions/account.contacts.map)" lists the people at an account by role, with their public profile and a checked work email. Aim for the person who feels the problem, the person who signs, and the person who has to make it work.

### 4. Watch for the right moment

A target account that isn't ready this month might be ready next month. "[Set up a company signal watch](/missions/account.watch.setup)" watches your accounts for the events you choose, like funding or a new leader. It needs approval, because it keeps running after you set it up.

"[Review signals on target companies](/missions/account.signals.review)" then gives you one list of what the watches found, ordered by score, so the team knows which account to work this week.

### 5. Reach out with something worth reading

Use what you learned. Name the change at their company and what you'd help with. "[Draft a first email to a lead](/missions/outreach.draft)" writes from the person's job title, their company and its news, and leaves the draft in your inbox. It never sends.

### 6. See who's paying attention

"[Identify companies visiting your site](/missions/company.visitors.identify)" names the companies behind your site traffic and keeps the ones that fit your ideal customer. It never names the people. A target account reading your pricing page is worth a call.

## What mistakes should you avoid?

- **Too many accounts.** If you can't name what changed at each one, the list is too long. Start small.
- **One contact per account.** If that person leaves or ignores you, the account goes quiet. Map three or more.
- **Generic messages to named accounts.** Writing "Hi {first name}" to a target account wastes the research.
- **Marketing and sales on different lists.** Agree on one list and one owner per account.
- **Judging it too early.** Big deals take time. In the first months, count replies and meetings at target accounts as well as closed deals.

## Questions teams ask about ABM

**How many accounts should we start with?**
As many as your team can research properly. For a small team, that might be 20 to 50.

**Is ABM only for big companies?**
No. Any team that sells to a known set of companies can use it, including a founder doing sales alone.

**Will it email people on its own?**
No. Emails come back as drafts. You read them and send them yourself.

**Do I need a developer?**
No. You ask in plain words, or your AI assistant asks for you.
