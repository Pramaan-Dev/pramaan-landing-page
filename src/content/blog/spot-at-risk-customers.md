If you work in customer success, you know how churn usually shows up: late. The renewal call comes, and only then do you learn the champion left, usage fell, and three tickets went unanswered. With Pramaan you pick an account and get one view of its usage, feedback, billing and tickets, with each observation tied to where it came from. You also see what could not be checked.

## Why do we notice unhappy customers so late?

Because the signals live in different places. Usage sits in your product. Payments sit in billing. Complaints sit in the help desk. Changes at the customer's company sit in the news.

No one has time to open all four for every account every week. So you check the loud accounts and miss the quiet ones. The quiet ones are often the ones that leave.

## What changes with Pramaan?

One request pulls the signals for one account into a single answer. Each observation names its source. Where Pramaan could not find something, it says so as a gap, rather than guessing. A second mission watches your important customer companies for changes that often come before trouble.

## How do I check one account, step by step?

The account below is made up, for illustration.

**You ask:** "Check how Northwind Traders is doing before their renewal next month." (Northwind Traders is a made-up name.)

**Pramaan plans.** It looks at which of your apps are connected: your product data, billing and help desk.

**It asks for approval.** The [Check a customer account's health](/missions/account.health) mission needs approval. It reads from your connected apps, so Pramaan shows you what it will read and waits for your approval before it starts.

**It reads the signals.** It looks at usage and feedback, billing records linked to the account, and the tickets you name. It checks the subscription and transaction history, so a failed or late payment shows up next to a drop in usage.

**Done, with proof.** You get a written summary. Each observation cites the record it came from. Gaps are listed plainly, for example "no usage data was available for this account." You decide what to do next: a call, a check-in, a discount conversation.

Pramaan does not email the customer, close tickets or change billing. It reports.

## How do I read the summary?

Read the gaps first. They tell you how much of the picture you have. Then read each observation next to its source, and open the record if something surprises you.

An account with a failed payment, an open ticket and no usage data is worth a call this week. An account with one slow-export complaint and healthy billing may only need a reply.

## How do I watch my key accounts for changes?

The [Watch companies for headcount changes](/missions/accounts.changes.watch) mission tracks headcount, funding, news and name changes at the companies you list. It runs as a standing monthly rental, so it keeps watching until you stop it. Ask for the price first, before you start it.

This is useful when the customer is a business. A funding round can mean a bigger budget. A drop in headcount can mean cuts, and cuts reach your contract. You hear about it when it happens, not at renewal.

## A simple routine you can copy

Pick your ten most important accounts. Once a week, ask for a health check on the two you know least about. Start the company watch for all ten. When a change comes in, check that account that day.

It replaces the habit of finding out at renewal.
