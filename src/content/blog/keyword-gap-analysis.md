If you run marketing or content, a keyword gap analysis shows the searches your rivals show up for and you don't. It turns "what should we write next?" into a ranked list. This guide explains what a keyword gap is, how to find yours, and how to turn the gaps into articles.

**Quick summary**

- A keyword gap is a search term your rivals rank for and your site doesn't.
- Pick the rivals you meet in search, which may differ from your sales rivals.
- Sort the gaps by what the searcher wants and how much they search, then pick the few you can win.
- Check the link gap too: sites that link to rivals but not to you.

## What is a keyword gap analysis?

A keyword gap analysis compares the search terms your rivals rank for with the ones you rank for, and lists the difference.

To rank means your page shows up in the search results for that term. The gap is every useful term where a rival's page shows up and yours doesn't.

Here's an example: a clinic scheduling company ranks for "online booking for clinics". Two rivals also rank for "how to reduce patient no-shows". The company has no page on no-shows. That term is a gap.

## Is a keyword gap the same as keyword research?

No. Keyword research finds every term people search on a topic. A keyword gap narrows that to the terms rivals already win.

The gap list is shorter and easier to act on, because a rival has already shown the term brings visits.

## Why run a keyword gap analysis?

- **You stop guessing what to write.** The list comes from searches people make, and rivals already rank for them.
- **You find proven demand.** If a rival ranks for a term, people search it and a page can rank for it.
- **You see where rivals are pulling ahead.** A growing gap often means a rival is investing in a topic.
- **You can plan the quarter.** A ranked gap list turns into a content calendar.

## How do you run a keyword gap analysis?

Work through it in five steps.

### 1. Pick the rivals you meet in search

Your search rivals are the sites that rank for the terms you want. They may be your sales rivals, or they may be review sites, blogs and directories.

"[Profile a competitor's search footprint](/missions/seo.competitor.profile)" shows one rival's search figures, the terms it ranks for, its ranking pages, its traffic by country, and the sites listed as its search competitors. That last part helps you find rivals you hadn't thought of.

### 2. Find the terms they rank for and you don't

Start from one seed term that describes your topic.

"[Find keywords rivals rank for, not you](/missions/keyword.gap)" takes that seed term, builds a set of related terms with their search volume, checks who ranks for each, and returns the terms where rivals rank and your site doesn't. Run it once per topic you care about.

### 3. Sort the gaps by what the searcher wants

A term like "what is a no-show" is someone learning. "No-show software pricing" is someone close to buying. Put the buying terms first, then the ones with the most searches.

"[Group keywords by intent](/missions/seo.keyword.cluster)" groups keyword ideas by what the searcher wants, with volume and difficulty kept alongside. Difficulty is an estimate of how hard a term is to rank for.

### 4. Check the link gap

Links from other sites help pages rank. If many sites link to a rival's page and none link to yours, writing a better page may not be enough.

"[Find sites linking to rivals but not you](/missions/links.gap)" lists sites that link to your rivals and don't appear among the links found to your site, with authority estimates if you ask. Those sites are your first outreach list.

### 5. Turn the best gaps into articles

Pick three to five gaps for the month. Write one strong page for each, aimed at what the searcher wants.

"[Plan an article from search data](/missions/content.brief)" builds an outline from the pages that already rank and the terms people search, with sections tied to that data.

## What mistakes should you avoid?

- **Comparing against the wrong rivals.** Your biggest sales rival may barely rank. Use the sites you meet in search.
- **Chasing every gap.** A list of 400 terms is a list nobody acts on. Pick a few each month.
- **Ignoring what the searcher wants.** A sales page won't rank for a "how to" search.
- **Forgetting links.** Some gaps are link gaps. A better page with no links may still sit below the rival.
- **Running it once.** Rivals publish every month. Re-run the gap each quarter.
