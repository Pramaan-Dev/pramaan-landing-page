If you work in legal or compliance, the first read of a contract is the same job every time. You hunt for the clauses that must be there and the phrases that should not be. Pramaan does that first pass against your own list and quotes each hit exactly, so you can go straight to the parts that need a person. It does not give legal advice, and a person makes every call.

## Why does a first pass take so long?

Most of a contract is routine, and you still have to read all of it to find the part that isn't. You search for termination, liability, renewal and governing law by eye, then copy the wording into your notes.

The risk is the clause you missed on page 14.

## What changes with Pramaan?

You write down what you require and what you flag. Pramaan reads the contract and checks it against that list. For each phrase it reports found or not found, with the exact words from the contract.

The list is yours, so the check follows your policy, not a generic one.

## How does it work, step by step?

**You ask:** "Check this supplier agreement. We require a termination-for-convenience clause and a data protection clause. Flag automatic renewal and unlimited liability."

**You supply the phrases.** The [Review a contract for risky phrases](/missions/contract.review) mission takes the contract text and two lists, the phrases you require and the phrases you consider risky.

**Pramaan reads the document.** It parses the file, extracts the text, and checks it against each phrase on your lists.

**Every quote is verified.** A quote only appears if the words are in the contract. If a phrase is not there, the result says so.

**You can ask for notes.** Optional notes explain a hit and name their sources. They are a starting point for your reading.

**You decide.** You open the contract at each hit, read the clause in context, and make the call. Pramaan narrows where to look. It does not judge whether a clause is acceptable.

## Questions legal teams ask

**Does it replace a lawyer?**
No. It does not give legal advice. It finds and quotes the words you asked about, and a person decides what they mean.

**Where do the quotes come from?**
From the contract you supplied. A quote is shown only when the words are in the text.

**Which countries does it cover?**
The mission checks the text you give it against the phrases you list. It does not apply any country's law, so what a clause means in your jurisdiction is a question for a person.

**Is my contract kept private?**
