If you run marketing for a brand, you know how creator hunting goes. You scroll hashtags, open profiles, squint at follower counts, and hope the likes are real. With Pramaan you name your niche, get a list of creators, check each one's audience and comments, and save a first message as a draft.

## Why is finding the right creators so slow?

Follower counts are easy to see and hard to trust. The useful signals sit in the comments and the engagement per post, and each platform shows them in its own way.

So a short list takes days. You open dozens of profiles in tabs, copy numbers into a sheet, and still wonder whether the comments came from real people.

## What changes with Pramaan?

You describe the niche once. Pramaan searches the platforms, reads each creator's own profile to confirm the match, then looks at audience numbers and recent comments for the ones you pick. It stops at a draft message. You send it.

## How does it work, step by step?

**You ask:** "Find creators in home coffee brewing and check which ones have real audiences."

**Pramaan plans.** It starts with [Find creators in a niche](/missions/creator.find). It runs each platform's own user search and keeps the rows that contain your niche's topic words. Each creator is looked up again in the platform's own profile read, and each row says whether that read confirmed it.

**You pick a few.** You choose the creators you care about from the list.

**It checks the audience.** [Check a creator's audience quality](/missions/creator.audience.check) reads the audience numbers on a creator's accounts and the comments on their recent posts. For each account it computes an engagement rate in code and shows the formula. For each post it shows how many people commented and gives a machine-written-text score. The score is an estimate, never proof. To get there, Pramaan reads recent posts and videos, the account profiles and the comments on each post, and runs a machine-written-text check on the comments.

**You read the signals.** Each signal comes with its number and a reading of notable, not notable or not checked. The mission gives no verdict on the creator, so you decide what the numbers mean for your brand.

**It drafts the message.** [Draft a message to a creator](/missions/creator.outreach.draft) writes an outreach note from the creator's public profile and your offer. Each sentence is checked against what was found, and the note is saved as a draft. It reads the creator's profile on each platform, looks for their other accounts, and writes the note from those.

**Done, with proof.** The numbers and comments come from the creators' own accounts, so you can open them and check.

## How do I read the comments myself?

Open the posts the report points to and look at who comments. If the same few accounts appear again and again, or the comments could sit under any post, you have learned something no follower count shows. If the comments refer to what is in the video, you have learned something too.

Treat a high machine-written-text score as a reason to open the comments, and nothing more.

## How do I phrase the ask?

Name the niche in the words people use on the platforms, such as home coffee brewing, and name the platforms you care about. A narrow niche returns a list you can read. A broad one returns rows you will never check.
