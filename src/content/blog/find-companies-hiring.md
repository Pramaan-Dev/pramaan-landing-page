If you sell something that helps a team that is hiring, a job posting is the best buying signal you can get. It tells you a company has money, a gap, and a deadline. With Pramaan you describe the kind of company and the role, and you get back companies that are hiring for it, with their open roles listed. You can then ask what a single company's hiring says about where it is heading.

## Why is finding hiring companies slow by hand?

Job boards list jobs. You search a role, scroll through postings, and then work out which employers are the right size and in the right market.

Then you repeat it next week, because the postings change. Most teams do it once, get a list, and let it go stale.

## What changes with Pramaan?

You describe the companies once. Pramaan finds the ones that match and are hiring for your role, and it picks out the open roles that fit. Then you can go deeper on any one account to see what it is hiring for and what that suggests.

## What does it look like, step by step?

**You ask:** "Find logistics software companies in Germany that are hiring a data engineer."

**Pramaan finds the companies.** The [Find leads at companies that are hiring](/missions/lead.hiring.find) mission searches for companies that match your description and are hiring for the role. It returns each company's open roles, with the matching ones picked out. In order, it searches for companies that match your description, searches jobs at each one, and fills in each company's profile beside the postings.

**You pick the accounts that look best.** Say you choose five.

**Pramaan reads the hiring for each one.** [See what a company is hiring for](/missions/company.hiring.signal) looks at the company's open roles and its recent company-page posts. It then gives a reading of the two: what the company seems to be building or fixing.

**You get a reason to call.** "Three open data roles and a post about a new warehouse" is a better opener than "I saw you're hiring."

## How do I turn a hiring list into a call?

Pick the accounts where the open role connects to what you sell. A company with three open data roles is putting effort into data work. A company with one stray posting may not be.

Then run the hiring reading on those accounts and open with something specific from it, such as the role and the post. If you need a named person to write to, ask for a contact at the company, then draft the first email and read it before it goes anywhere.

## What does the hiring reading cover?

It reads two things: the company's open roles and its recent company-page posts. A written reading then ties them together, so a list of roles and a post about a new site are read as one story. It states what the roles and posts suggest. It does not predict anything.

## How do I narrow a long list?

Start with a tight description and read the first results before you widen it. If the list comes back short, loosen one condition at a time, region first and size second. That way you know which condition cost you the companies.

## How should I describe the role?

Use the words a posting would use. "Data engineer" works better than "someone who builds pipelines."

Name the market too: country, industry and rough size. A tight description gives a short list you can act on. A loose one gives you hundreds of rows you'll never get through.
