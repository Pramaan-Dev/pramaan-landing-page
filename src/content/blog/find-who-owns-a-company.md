If you handle due diligence in legal or compliance, you often need to know who stands behind a company before you sign with it. Pramaan maps the owners, officers and linked entities that the company has disclosed, then lists its court and insolvency record as facts. A person reads the result and makes the call. It is research support, not legal advice.

## Why is finding the real owner so hard?

Ownership sits in layers. A company is owned by another company, which has partners, which have officers, and each layer is a separate register search.

The work is slow, and it is easy to stop one layer too early.

## What changes with Pramaan?

You name the company. Pramaan searches the registries and assembles one tree of controllers, shareholders, partners, officers and linked entities. It then adds the company's legal record, listed fact by fact.

The map shows what has been disclosed. If an owner is hidden or undisclosed, the map will not show it.

## How does it work, step by step?

**You ask:** "Who owns Harbor Lane Trading, and has it been in court?" (Harbor Lane Trading is a made-up name for this example.)

**Pramaan maps the owners.** The [Map who owns a company](/missions/company.ownership.map) mission lists owners and shareholdings, officers, contributions and the wider network around the company. It also lists registry statements and securities where they exist.

**You read the tree.** Each node shows who it is and how it connects. Follow the links upward until you reach a person, or a point where disclosure stops.

**Pramaan looks up the legal record.** The [Look up a company's legal record](/missions/company.legal.record) mission lists court records, insolvency proceedings, registered charges, the history of those charges and related-party transactions. It reports them as facts, with no verdict attached.

**You review the two together.** Check whether a named owner appears in the legal record, and whether a registered charge matches what the company told you.

**You decide.** A person decides whether to proceed, ask more questions, or escalate. Pramaan does not rate the company as safe or unsafe.

## What does the map not show?

It shows what registries disclose. Ownership held through undisclosed arrangements, or in places with limited public registers, can leave gaps. Treat a gap as a question to ask the company, not as a finding.

A court record that matches a name is not proof of the same party. The [Search court records by name](/missions/party.court-records.search) mission returns possible matches and does not assert that a party is the same person, so keep that caution when you read any name match.

## Questions legal teams ask

**Does it replace a lawyer?**
No. It gathers and organizes public records. A person interprets them and decides what to do.

**Where do the sources come from?**
From company registries and court records. Each result names where it came from.

**Which countries does it cover?**
Coverage depends on the registry. Run a quote or a small test on the company you care about before relying on it.

**What if an owner is not listed?**
Then the map has nothing to show for that owner. Ask the company directly, and note the gap in your file.
