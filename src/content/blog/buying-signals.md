If you work in sales or marketing, buying signals tell you which companies might be ready to buy right now. This guide explains what counts as a signal, which ones are worth your time, and how to turn a signal into a conversation before a competitor does.

**Quick summary**

- A buying signal is a dated event that makes a company more likely to need what you sell, like new funding, a new leader or a hiring push.
- Pick three to five signals that showed up before your best deals, and ignore the rest.
- Check every signal against your ideal customer profile. A signal at a bad-fit company is still a bad lead.
- Act fast, reach the right person, and say why you're writing now.

## What are buying signals?

A buying signal is something a company does, with a date on it, that hints it may soon need your product.

Common ones:

- **Funding**: a new round means new budget and new goals.
- **Hiring**: open roles show where a company is putting money and people.
- **Leadership change**: a new head of sales or operations often reviews the tools the team uses.
- **Launches and expansion**: a new product, office or market brings new problems to solve.
- **Technology change**: a company starts or stops using software that sits next to yours.

Here's an example for a company that sells sales training: "A software company raised a Series A last month and has posted six sales roles since." That company is building a sales team, and new teams need training.

## Are buying signals the same as intent data?

No. Buying signals are public events at a company. Intent data is a guess about what a company is researching, based on what its staff read online.

The two work well together, but a signal you can link to gives you something real to say in the first line of an email.

## Why do buying signals matter?

Because a good-fit company with no reason to change will not move, however good your pitch.

- **You write at the right moment.** A company that just raised money is setting budgets now, not next year.
- **Your first line writes itself.** "Congrats on the new London office" beats "I hope this finds you well."
- **Reps stop guessing.** The list each morning is the companies where something just changed.
- **Marketing gets a sharper target.** Ads and content can follow the same events sales is watching.

## How do you find and act on buying signals?

In six steps: choose your signals, find the companies, keep watching, rank them, find the person, and reach out.

### 1. Choose the signals that matter to you

Look at your last 10 to 20 won deals. What happened at each company in the months before they bought? Pick the three to five events that keep coming up. If you have an [ideal customer profile](/blog/ideal-customer-profile), its "buying triggers" row is your starting list.

### 2. Find companies showing those signals now

Ask for the event in plain words: "fintech companies in the UK that raised money in the last 90 days." "[Find companies showing buying signals](/missions/company.signals.find)" looks for companies with dated signals and checks each one against your ideal customer.

For one company, "[See what a company is hiring for](/missions/company.hiring.signal)" reads its open roles and recent company-page posts, then says what the two suggest.

### 3. Keep watching your target accounts

Signals keep coming after your first search. "[Set up a company signal watch](/missions/account.watch.setup)" creates a watch on the companies and events you care about. It needs approval, because a watch keeps running after you set it up.

### 4. Rank what comes in

Not every signal deserves a call today. "[Review signals on target companies](/missions/account.signals.review)" reads what your watches found, with each company's profile and any drafts you already have, and gives you one list ordered by score. Work from the top.

### 5. Find the right person

A signal points at a company. You still need a person. "[Map the contacts at a company](/missions/account.contacts.map)" lists people by role, with their public profile and a checked work email. A new head of sales is often the person to write to first.

### 6. Reach out while it's fresh

Name the signal in your first line, and say what you'd help with. "[Run outreach from a buying signal](/missions/outreach.signal.campaign)" does the whole run: it finds companies from a plain signal question, picks the right contacts at each, checks their emails and holds a draft for each contact. Nothing is sent.

## What mistakes should you avoid?

- **Chasing every signal.** Twenty signal types means reps act on none. Keep it to the few that came before real deals.
- **Ignoring fit.** A funding round at a company you can't serve is noise. Check fit before timing.
- **Waiting too long.** A signal goes stale. If it's a month old, someone else has probably called.
- **Opening with the signal and nothing else.** "Congrats on the round" is not a reason to reply. Say what changes for them and how you help.
- **Writing to the company, not a person.** Find the one person the event affects most.
