If you're a founder or you run finance, you've had the invoice from a supplier you've never heard of. Before you pay, you want to know if the business is real. With Pramaan you name the supplier and get a check against the official register, the domain record, and a short overview with sources.

## Why is it hard to check a supplier before paying?

No single place tells you. The register is on one site, the domain record on another, and the news on a third. Many people skip the check because it takes half an hour they don't have.

That is how payments go to the wrong account.

## What changes with Pramaan?

You give Pramaan the supplier's name and website. It looks at the country's official register, reads the domain record and DNS, checks the web address, and searches the news. You get findings with their evidence, so you can see where each came from.

## How does it work, step by step?

**You ask:** "Is this supplier a real company? Here is their name and website."

**Pramaan plans.** It starts with [Verify a business is real](/missions/company.verify). It checks whether the business is on its country's official register and whether the domain looks like its own. Each finding comes with its evidence.

**You ask for risk signals.** [Check a supplier for risk](/missions/vendor.risk.check) reads the register, the domain record, DNS, a verdict on the web address and recent news. It ends with a factual assessment.

**You ask for the first look.** [Take a first look at a company](/missions/company.diligence) gives you the register record, domain registration, news, links, site authority and stock facts, with a written overview.

**Done, with proof.** You can open the register entry yourself, so the check doesn't rest on trusting a summary.

Start with the first. It is the narrowest question: is this business on the register, and does the domain look like its own? If the answer is yes, the other two fill in the picture. If the answer is no, you have your reason to pause the payment.

## When should I run these checks?

Run them before the first payment to any supplier you haven't used before. Run them again before you sign a larger contract, because the register, the domain record and the news can all change.

When a supplier sends new bank details, use the payee check as well. It looks at the bank or payment address, and it needs your approval before it runs.

Keep the result with the invoice. If someone asks later why you paid, the register link and the domain record are already attached.

## Questions founders and finance teams ask

**Do I need a developer?**
No. You ask in plain words, or your AI assistant asks for you.

**Can it move money?**
No. These missions only read records.

**Which countries does it cover?**
It checks the official register of the supplier's country. Coverage depends on the country, so the result says what it could and couldn't find.

**Is there a record of what was checked?**
Each finding shows its evidence, so you can keep it with the invoice.

**Who approves payments?**
You do. These missions never pay anyone. The separate payee check, which looks at bank or payment details, needs approval before it runs.

**Does a clean result mean the supplier is safe?**
No. It means the records you can check line up. Missing records are reported as missing.
