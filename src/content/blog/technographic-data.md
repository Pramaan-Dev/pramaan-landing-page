If you work in sales or marketing, the software a company runs tells you a lot about whether it will buy from you. Technographic data is that information: the tools and technology a company uses. This guide explains what it is, where it comes from, and how to use it to find better prospects and reach them at the right time.

**Quick summary**

- Technographic data lists the software a company uses, like its online store, its email tool or its analytics.
- Most of it is read from the company's own website. Job posts fill in some of what the website doesn't show.
- Use it to find companies on tools you plug into or replace, and to spot when a company adds or drops a tool.
- It's one input. Combine it with company size, industry and timing.

## What is technographic data?

Technographic data is information about the technology a company uses: which tools, in which categories, and sometimes which versions.

Most of it comes from looking at a company's website. The code a site loads gives away a lot: its online store platform, its chat widget, its analytics, its payment provider. Open roles add more. A job post asking for "three years of Salesforce" is a strong hint about the CRM.

An example: "Runs Shopify for its store, Klaviyo for email, and is hiring a HubSpot admin."

## How is technographic data different from firmographic data?

Firmographic data describes what a company is. Technographic data describes what it runs.

## Why does technographic data matter?

Because the tools a company already runs often decide if your product fits. Four reasons it helps:

- **Easier sales.** A company already on a tool yours connects to has less to change.
- **Clear replacement targets.** If you replace a rival's product, every company running it is a prospect.
- **Better timing.** A company that just added or dropped a tool is often making decisions now.
- **Sharper messages.** "Here's how we work with your Shopify store" beats a generic pitch.

## How do you use technographic data, step by step?

Five steps, from a list of tools to timed outreach.

### 1. Pick the tools that matter to you

Write down three kinds of tool: the ones your product plugs into, the ones it replaces, and the ones that hint at a need. A store with no reviews app might need one. Keep the list short.

### 2. See what one company runs

Start with an account you know. "[See what technology a company uses](/missions/company.techstack)" reads the technologies detected on the company's website, adds its open roles, and writes a short view of the two together. Check it against what you already know about that company.

### 3. Find companies that run a tool

Now go wide. "[Find companies using a technology](/missions/lead.techstack.find)" finds companies that use a named tool and confirms each one by detecting the tool on that company's own website. The ones it couldn't confirm are listed apart, so you know which to trust.

### 4. Check how widely a tool is used

Before you build a campaign around one tool, check its reach. "[Report on a technology's market share](/missions/tech.market.report)" shows how many sites use a technology, by country, language, category and how popular the site is, and which other tools they use with it.

### 5. Watch for changes

A change is often the best reason to reach out. "[See what technology a site added or dropped](/missions/site.stack.changes)" lists the tools a site added or removed since a date you pick, checked against a fresh scan of the site.

## How do you put technographic data to work with Pramaan?

Technographic data is most useful when it feeds into the rest of the sales job:

- **Use a tool as a fit check.** "[Qualify a company against your criteria](/missions/lead.qualify)" takes up to eight written checks, like "Uses Shopify", and answers each one met, not met or unknown, with the evidence it used.
- **Compare accounts.** "[Compare companies side by side](/missions/company.compare)" puts two to five companies next to each other, including the technologies each runs.
- **Find the person to talk to.** "[Build a list of people to contact](/missions/lead.list.build)" finds people by role at the companies you pick. See [How sales teams build a lead list without the spreadsheet](/blog/lead-list-without-the-spreadsheet).

## What mistakes should you avoid?

- **Trusting "nothing detected" too much.** An empty result means nothing showed on the website. Tools used only inside the company, like payroll, rarely show there.
- **Using old data.** Companies change tools. Check for changes before you build a campaign on last year's list.
- **Picking prospects by tools alone.** A company on the right tool can still be the wrong size or in the wrong industry.
- **Opening with "I saw you use X."** Lead with the problem you solve for teams on that tool.
- **Tracking too many tools.** Ten tools that matter beat a hundred that don't.

## Questions teams ask about technographic data

**Where does technographic data come from?**
Mostly from the company's own website, which shows the tools it loads. Job posts add tools the website doesn't show.

**Can it see the software a company uses internally?**
Only some of it. Internal tools rarely show on a website, though job posts often mention them.

**Do I need a developer?**
No. You ask in plain words, or your AI assistant asks for you.

**How fresh is it?**
Each detected tool comes with when it was last seen. To check for recent changes, the change lookup compares against a fresh scan.
