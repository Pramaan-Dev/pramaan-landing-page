If you run hiring, you know the moment before a job goes live. Someone asks, "What should we offer?" You open a few salary sites, find three different numbers, and pick one that feels safe. With Pramaan you name the role and the place, and you get pay figures from several sources side by side, plus a count of how many people could fill the job.

## Why is it hard to know what to pay?

Because every source measures something different. One site shows advertised ranges. Another shows what employees report. A third shows a trend over time.

Copy a number from each into a spreadsheet and they look comparable. They are not. Some are yearly, some hourly, some from ten postings, some from ten thousand. Post too low and the good candidates scroll past. Post too high and you've reset what the whole team expects to pay.

## What changes with Pramaan?

You get each source's figures separately, with its own unit, sample size and date. Pramaan does not average them into one tidy number. You see where the sources agree, where they don't, and how much evidence sits behind each, then you set the range.

## How do I benchmark pay for one role, step by step?

Here is the flow, with a made-up role. The role and the city are examples only.

**You ask:** "We're hiring a customer support lead in Manchester. What does the role pay, and how many people could we reach?"

**Pramaan plans.** It splits the question in two. First, what the role pays. Second, how large the pool is.

**It checks advertised pay.** The [Benchmark pay for a job](/missions/job.salary.benchmark) mission keeps three things apart: salary thresholds, pay history, and the ranges employers advertised in job posts. It also reports how many postings it looked at, so you know whether a range rests on a handful of ads or a large sample.

**It compares the other sources.** The [Compare salary sources](/missions/salary.benchmark) mission puts several salary sources next to each other. Each keeps its own units, sample and date. If two sources disagree, you see both.

**It sizes the pool.** The [Size your talent pool](/missions/candidate.pool.size) mission counts how many professionals match your search. It also returns a small sample with names masked, so you can check that the search found the right kind of person, and the advertised pay thresholds for that group.

**Done, with proof.** You get the figures, the sample sizes behind them and the sources, in one answer. You decide what to offer.

None of these three missions needs approval. They only read data. Nothing is posted, sent or paid out.

## How do I read the numbers once I have them?

Start with the sample sizes. A range built from many postings tells you more than one built from a few. Then look at the gap between advertised pay and the other sources. A wide gap is worth a conversation before you post.

Then look at the pool. A small pool at your planned pay is a warning. You may need to raise the range, widen the location, or accept a longer search.

Write down the range, the sources behind it and the date. When someone later asks why you offered that figure, you have the answer.
