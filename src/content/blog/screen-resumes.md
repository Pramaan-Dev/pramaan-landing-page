If you do the hiring, a pile of resumes eats your week. You read the same lines again and again to find out whether someone has what the role asks for. With Pramaan you list the requirements, and each resume comes back with quotes that show where it meets each one. A person makes every decision. Pramaan gathers the evidence.

## Why does screening resumes take so long?

Each resume has to be read against the same list, and the evidence is buried in different places. One person lists skills at the top. Another mentions them in a job description from three years ago.

The longer the pile, the more tired the reading gets, and the first few resumes shape how you read the rest.

## What changes with Pramaan?

You state the requirements. For each one, Pramaan looks in one resume and returns a quote when it finds one. Nothing is added up, scored or ranked. Each requirement reads evidenced, not evidenced or not checked, and a person reads it and decides.

## How do I screen a resume against requirements?

Use the [Screen a resume against requirements](/missions/resume.screen) mission. It takes one resume (a link, or the file itself) and 1 to 10 requirements. It runs once per resume.

**You ask:** "Screen this resume against: three years of TypeScript, and experience running a team of five." (made-up requirements)

**Pramaan plans.** The mission has two steps. It reads the document, whether it's a PDF, an Office file or an image, and then pulls out the quoted evidence for each requirement.

**You see the proof.** For each requirement you get one of three answers, with the quoted line from the resume when there is one:

<table>
<tr><th>Answer</th><th>What it means</th></tr>
<tr><td>evidenced</td><td>A quote in the resume supports the requirement.</td></tr>
<tr><td>not evidenced</td><td>No supporting quote was found. This is not a verdict on the person.</td></tr>
<tr><td>not checked</td><td>The requirement could not be checked.</td></tr>
</table>

There is no total and no ranking. You read the evidence for each requirement yourself. This mission does not need approval, and it does not contact the candidate.

## What can I put in the requirements?

Skills, experience and qualifications only. Pramaan refuses a requirement that names or implies a personal trait, such as age, gender, family status, nationality, religion, disability, appearance, name, native language, address or career gaps. The refusal is a guardrail on what you can ask. It does not make the result unbiased, so a person still has to read the evidence with care.

## How do I check my job ad before the resumes arrive?

Use the [Audit your job ad](/missions/job.posting.audit) mission. You give the ad's text, the role title and a country. It reviews the ad's clarity against a sourced rubric, and it reads advertised-pay data for comparison, kept separate from the clarity review. You can turn off the market comparison or the clarity review.

A clearer ad often means a better-matched pile of resumes. This mission does not need approval.

## Questions hiring teams ask

**Is screening fair?**
Pramaan does not claim it is. It returns quotes against your stated requirements and refuses personal traits as requirements. A person makes every decision.

**Does it rank candidates?**
No. It adds nothing up and orders nothing.

**Where does candidate data go?**
The resume is read to produce the result you asked for. Ask us for the current data handling details before you use it on real candidates.

**Can it run payroll?**
A payroll summary mission exists, [`payroll.summary`](/missions/payroll.summary), and it needs approval, so it waits for your approval before it runs. The resume mission does nothing with pay.

**Do I need a developer?**
No. You ask in plain words, or your AI assistant asks for you.
