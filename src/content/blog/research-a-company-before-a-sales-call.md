If you carry a quota, you know the scramble before a call. LinkedIn in one tab, the company site in another, a news search, a funding page, and a half-finished note. With Pramaan you name the company and get one sourced profile back, plus a brief on the founders if it's a startup. Each part tells you where it came from, so you can check a claim before you say it out loud.

## Why does call prep eat so much of a rep's day?

Because the facts live in different places. The register record is on one site, the news on another, the hiring page on a third, and the founders' backgrounds somewhere else again.

A rep who walks in with three real facts sounds prepared. A rep who spent the prep time hunting for them has less time left to sell. Most prep ends up shallow because the clock runs out.

## What changes with Pramaan?

You ask once, in plain words, and Pramaan gathers the pieces into one answer. You get a company profile with sources, a founder brief when the account is a startup, and a snapshot of recent changes if you want to know what moved this month.

## What does it look like, step by step?

**You ask:** "Research Northwind Freight before my call on Thursday. Tell me who runs it and what changed lately." (Northwind Freight is a made-up company, used here as an example.)

**Pramaan researches the company.** The [Research a company](/missions/company.research) mission starts from the company's name or domain. It returns one sourced profile: the register record, recent news, pages from the company's own site, its social page, and a written summary. Open roles and similar companies are part of the reading too. Behind that one answer are eleven steps. Pramaan enriches the company, searches the official register, reads recent news, maps and reads pages on its site, reads its social page, detects the technology the site runs, searches open roles, finds similar companies, and writes the summary.

**Pramaan briefs you on the founders.** For a startup, [Brief on a startup's founders](/missions/startup.founders.brief) builds a sales-call brief. It covers the founders, their public profiles, and the company's funding history and the founders' own investments, kept separate so you don't mix them up. It lists the founders, enriches each person, and pulls the funding history, the founders' own investments and recent news.

**Pramaan snapshots recent changes.** [Snapshot recent changes at a company](/missions/company.changes.snapshot) pulls dated news, posts and job openings, checks that they belong to the right company, and writes a summary grounded in what it found. This one needs approval, because the mission can also set up a schedule. It waits for your approval before it runs.

**You walk in ready.** You read one page instead of switching between tabs, and you can open the source behind any line you plan to quote.

## What does a good call-prep ask look like?

Name the company, say when the call is, and say what you want to know. "Who runs it, what did they announce this quarter, and are they hiring?" gives Pramaan a clear job.

If you only have a name, that works. If you have the domain, use it. A domain avoids mix-ups between companies with similar names.

## How do I use the profile on the call?

Open with one dated item from the changes snapshot, such as a new post or a job opening, and ask what is behind it. If the founder brief lists funding, keep the company's rounds and the founders' own investments apart when you talk, as the brief does. Check any line you plan to quote against its source before the call.

## Questions reps ask

**Do I need a developer?**
No. You ask in plain words, or your agent asks for you.

**Where do the facts come from?**
From the company's official register, news, its own website and public profiles. The profile names its sources, so you can check them.

**Will it contact anyone?**
No. Research missions read and report. Anything that sends a message or sets something up waits for your approval.

**What if it can't find something?**
The answer shows what it found and what it didn't. A gap in the profile is more useful than a guess you repeat to a prospect.

**Does it work with our CRM?**
Some missions do. [Brief on a CRM deal](/missions/deal.brief) reads a checked deal in a connected CRM, its linked notes and optional company news, and writes a cited brief. It needs approval.
